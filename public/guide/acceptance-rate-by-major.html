Acceptance rates by major, not by university

Every ranking site publishes one acceptance rate per university. For a student choosing a major, that number is close to useless — you apply to a programme, and programmes inside the same university can differ by a factor of five.

Why this happens

Large universities admit into colleges, not into the university as a whole. A computer science applicant at a school with a direct-admit CS programme is competing against other CS applicants for a fixed number of seats, not against the general pool. The published rate averages the competitive programmes with the quiet ones, so it describes nobody.

The effect is strongest in computer science, business, nursing and engineering — the four fields where applications have grown fastest. It is weakest in the humanities and in universities that admit undeclared.

What to do with this

  • Check whether your target school admits directly into your major. If it does, the published rate is not your rate.
  • Ask whether internal transfer is open before planning to switch after arrival. At most schools with direct admission it is capped and competitive.
  • Do not pick a subject for its admit rate. Four years is a long time to study something chosen on a spreadsheet, and admissions readers can tell.
